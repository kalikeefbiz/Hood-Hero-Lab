# Character pipeline evaluation

## Decision

**Test A is the strongest near-term option.** The retargeted run adds visible weight transfer and much more even left/right toe speeds while retaining the approved face, body and starter outfit. Its current jog speed, contact variability and loop seam still need production tuning. The existing walk already has consistent planted-toe speed. Test A does not justify replacing every current clip as a bundle.

**MPFB is the stronger anatomical starting point.** It produces a continuous, cleanly weighted surface with consistent facial and hand topology and costs no generation credits. In this trial, the MPFB rig provided no decisive locomotion advantage from the same source. The close-up identity reads as a different person, its long-fingered hands remain stiff, and the reused outfit shows degraded collar and sleeve fit. Keep MPFB as an anatomy and modular wardrobe R&D path. [Front](../renders/front.png) · [Side](../renders/side.png) · [Close-up](../renders/close-up.png) · [Run](../renders/run.mp4)

## A/B controls and measured motion

The baseline and Test A share the same glTF mesh, skin, inverse binds, node hierarchy and original buffers. The preview binds the same face-01, complexion-01 and garment palettes on both. Test B changes body, material and rig together, so the comparison can isolate the effect of changing animation on the canonical body and can compare the two foundations, but cannot attribute every B difference to anatomy alone. Test B uses MPFB’s A-pose game rig with fitted fingers. The canonical T-pose rig’s wide knee/ankle stance, body-specific shoulder and elbow pivots, and different hand structure would require a new bind plus skinning to reuse on this body. A bind comparison is visual evidence of divergence. [Bind-pose render](../renders/bind.png) · [Baseline receipt](../baseline/README.md)

The estimates below are medians of backward toe-joint velocity in near-floor intervals at 30 Hz. “Near floor” means within 28 mm of each toe's minimum height in that clip. Toe roll and threshold choice affect this proxy. It measures the in-place motion presented to Three.js and is suitable for comparing gait symmetry. A true contact speed needs heel and toe contact classification on the skinned soles, and a residual sliding check after applying virtual or actual ground translation. Hip excursion is the vertical span of the mapped hip joint.

| Clip | Source trajectory | Baseline L / R | Test A L / R | MPFB L / R |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Idle | ≈0 m/s | ≈0 / ≈0 | ≈0 / ≈0 | ≈0 / ≈0 |
| Walk | 0.86 m/s | 0.81 / 0.81 m/s | 0.78 / 0.78 m/s | 0.84 / 0.84 m/s |
| Run | 1.87 m/s | 1.20 / 2.42 m/s | 1.69 / 1.68 m/s | 1.64 / 1.82 m/s |

- **Idle and balance.** The original run and idle have under 2 mm of hip elevation change. Test A adds 78 mm in run and keeps the feet quiet in idle. The new idle is comparatively still, with left wrist travel fore/aft at 17 mm versus 52 mm for baseline, near-straight knees, open fingers and a more symmetrical stance. The baseline's relaxed wrist gesture and asymmetry are worth retaining as human features. Test A remains a quieter idle with an open-hand posture that needs polish. [Idle comparison](../renders/idle.mp4)
- **Walk.** The mocap shortens fore/aft left wrist travel from 368 mm to 203 mm on the canonical rig, and lets the left elbow extend within about 1–15° flexion versus 21–37° in the existing walk. The leg motion includes more extension at contact. This reads as a smaller, more casual swing and a lighter bend in the arms. The baseline already plants evenly by the toe proxy. The new motion's hip elevation span is 32 mm, versus 52 mm for baseline. The MPFB shoulders still round visibly when the arm swings forward. [Walk comparison](../renders/walk.mp4)
- **Run.** The baseline’s toe-speed asymmetry and nearly fixed hip height are visible in the side loop. A improves both measurements while showing heavier elbow flexion (102–127° on the left, versus 80–101°) and shorter hand travel (112 mm versus 364 mm). MPFB shows 85 mm hip excursion, similar elbow motion and a 17 cm/s difference between the sampled toes. Close contact is variable in both retargets. Test A’s left toe p10–p90 spans roughly 0.65–1.81 m/s, including toe roll and seam motion. The lowest sole is near zero in both and the peak lowest-sole heights are about 16 mm for A and 39 mm for MPFB. These are in-place jog samples, and neither is calibrated for the full city run speed. [Run comparison](../renders/run.mp4)
- **Head, neck and back.** The unadjusted capture pitches downward about 18–20° even in idle. The common gaze adjustment puts it at about 3–5° down on both rigs in idle and 12–17° down during the jog. The side shots still show the head ahead of the shoulder line on both rigs. The existing broad shoulder silhouette and texture artifacts around the eyes remain in A with new clips, so those cannot be fixed by changing the animation. MPFB brings more even topology and independent eyes, yet the forehead/scalp albedo is smooth and the face and complexion differ from the supplied reference. It needs identity sculpting and texture calibration. Upright balance here means pose and contact appearance. Center of mass was not calculated. [Source capture before retarget](../renders/source-capture.png) · [Side](../renders/side.png) · [Close-up](../renders/close-up.png)
- **Loops and transitions.** All six replacement loops close to the measured joint position precision and Blender Skills reports no endpoint pops. Idle has zero hitch flags. Walk and run still have seam speed dips even after matching adjacent seam tangents. The viewer uses a 200 ms crossfade. Production rate matching and phase-aware transitions remain open. The existing controller clamps walk at 1.6× and run at 1.4×. A 1.69 m/s jog reaches only about 2.37 m/s of clip-equivalent travel at the run clamp, while the controller can reach 4.2 m/s. Feeding this jog straight into that slot would add sliding. Run-loop rate tuning needs a faster source or an approved jog/sprint split. No live-game playback measurement was made. All runtime derivatives use Meshopt, quantized geometry, KTX2 images at 1024 px or below, 30 Hz key grids, Idle/Walk/Run, a stationary root and no new scale tracks. The four tested body packages have zero unweighted runtime vertices and no more than four nonzero influences per vertex. The GLB validator reported zero errors. Warnings include KTX2 recognition, generated tangent space on the refitted outfit and an existing skinned node parenting warning. Three.js rendered the KTX2 assets. Meshopt quantization emits separate inverse-bind palettes for B’s mesh parts on a shared 53-node hierarchy. Its base GLB meets the byte and triangle budgets; the bundled clothing demonstration deliberately includes two body meshes and exceeds the production body file-size budget. Split outfit resources by slot and share an index-mask remap before using a similar delivery on mobile. [GLB verification](runtime-glb.json) · [Browser draw counts](browser.json)

The merged production checker passes the original delivery with zero problems. Its experimental invocation stops at the production layout gate on Test B. The pilot uses the separate ID and sources documented in its manifest. [Production checker on MPFB](production-check-mpfb.txt) Under Blender Skills game-profile QA, the canonical source body has four degenerate UV faces, one degenerate face, seven coplanar triangle pairs and substantial disconnected/split topology. MPFB’s evaluated skin is a single island with 13,380 vertices, no degenerate faces, duplicate vertices, flagged overlapping coplanar triangles or zero-area UVs. The authored source has 981 vertices above four influences. Blender normalizes the strongest four at export. The topology results show a concrete MPFB advantage, while visible joint polish and stable mobile budgets still need model review. [Canonical mesh report](mesh-a.txt) · [MPFB mesh report](mesh-b.txt) · [MPFB rig report](rig-b.txt)

The preview was exercised in Chromium software WebGL at desktop and 390 × 844. Touch emulation confirmed single-model selection, skeleton and clothing switches, and a measurable Idle→Walk crossfade. The outfit versions rendered six calls each including the ground grid. The day view reveals most surface issues. At gameplay distance they are less conspicuous, but the rounded shoulder and collar silhouette still read. These counts measure rendering work, not iPhone frame rate, memory or thermal behavior. Linux WebKit could not launch because required GTK/graphics libraries were missing. Physical Safari device validation remains open. ## Existing outfit transfer

On the current foundation, a bone-name remap and the original hash-bound triangle mask load all three original garment GLBs. The tee and jeans retain their silhouette in motion. There is a small boot/cuff reveal and occasional finger/hip overlap. The sneakers sit below the barefoot plane and the preview raises the fitted model 23 mm with the outfit on.

The MPFB copy preserves garment patterns, UVs and cotton/denim/leather material inputs. The fitting script reshapes the sleeves around the A-pose arms, maps waist and leg landmarks, repositions the sneakers, interpolates the target skin weights and derives a separate body mask. The average per-vertex displacement is about 81 mm for the tee, 74 mm for the jeans and 70 mm for the sneakers. The tee peaks at 202 mm, reflecting the arm angle change. Existing inverse binds and body-mask ordinal ranges cannot be carried across. The fitted outfit is recognizable at gameplay distance but the collar has visible skin intrusion, the arms show irregular trim around the sleeve during some poses, and the shirt hem rides high. Refit the collar, sleeve openings and waist as localized mesh edits, then regenerate masks with coverage margin and recheck intersections in motion. Jeans and footwear are closer to reusable. [Fit displacement](wardrobe-fit.json) · [Front](../renders/front.png) · [Close-up](../renders/close-up.png)

Estimated additional manual polish after the automated first fit on a comparable variant is 1–2 hours for the tee, 0.75–1.5 hours for the jeans, 0.5–1 hour for the shoes and 0.5–1 hour for the assembled motion review. These are planning estimates for this three-piece outfit. Garments with open fronts, hood attachments or closer tailoring require independent fit review. The rest of the catalog was not sampled.

## Tools and cost

- **Cost measured** — $0 and zero Meshy credits. The current merged baseline reports 45 historical Meshy credits across its two faces. Actual labor and credit price for the earlier delivery are unavailable. The A route avoids new anatomy spending. B avoids new anatomy and rig generation fees and adds identity and wardrobe labor. [Canonical source manifest](../../protagonist-male/manifest.json)
- **Automation** — a timed rerun with cached inputs took 1 min 58 s to verify inputs, build A/B/C, compress and validate the GLBs. That excludes first download, research, script authoring, visual iteration and video capture. Use the [rebuild script](../pipeline/rebuild.sh) for repeated runs. A practical planning allowance is 1–3 hours to curate and tune new movement once source/licensing is established, plus 2–5 hours for an art-directed MPFB head and texture pass before wardrobe. These are estimates, not throughput benchmarks.
- **Versions** — Blender 5.3.0 (02f0899d78e3), MPFB 2.0.18, Blender Skills 1.3.3 at the latest inspected revision 1a1cc6c, Node 24.22.3, Three.js 0.170, glTF-Transform 4.5.1, Meshoptimizer 1.3.0, KTX2 Encoder 0.6.0. The pilot used Blender Skills' rest-space method and its unmodified rig, mesh and loop checks. The game-dev CLI was unavailable. [Build inputs and revision hashes](../licensing/provenance.json)
- **Authoring routes** — CharacterCreator shows a viable MPFB-to-GLB path and how morph-driven joint offsets can preserve rig fit as proportions change. Its interactive morph/cloth architecture and procedural clips were outside this controlled comparison. The official MPFB Bridge starts and answers status in Blender 5.3. The pilot uses MPFB’s direct Python services for reproducible background batch builds.
